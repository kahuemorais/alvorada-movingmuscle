## How many panels does a house need?

The count follows from two numbers: how much electricity the house uses, and how much a single panel makes where the house is. In Phoenix, a 450-watt panel produces about 70 kilowatt hours in a month, so a three-bedroom house with a $220 bill needs 17 panels to cover 80% of what it uses.

## What does one panel produce?

A panel's monthly output is its wattage times the peak sun hours the location averages, times the days in the month, times a performance factor that covers heat, dust and inverter losses. Phoenix averages 6.5 peak sun hours a day, which is among the highest in the country, so the arithmetic on a 450-watt panel runs 450 watts x 6.5 hours x 30 days x 0.8, which is 70.2 kilowatt hours a month.

## What changes the count?

Four things, and only one of them is about the panel:

- The monthly bill, because usage is what the system replaces
- How much of that bill the owner wants to cover
- The sunlight hours where the house is, which move the output of every panel
- The wattage of the panel chosen, which decides how many are needed to reach the same total

A house in a cloudier state with the same bill needs more panels than a house in Phoenix, because each panel produces less there.

## Why is there a minimum size?

Every installation has a floor. The Phoenix page uses 8 panels as the minimum, so a small condo with a $90 bill ends up with 8 panels even though 6 would cover its usage. That floor is the reason a small household takes longer to pay the system off than a large one: it is buying more capacity than it uses.

## Does a bigger bill mean more panels?

Yes, in the same city and at the same coverage. On the Phoenix page the four household profiles land like this at 80% coverage: a $220 bill takes 17 panels, a $310 bill takes 24, and a $430 bill with a pool and an electric car takes 33. The same profile covered at 100% takes more panels still, because it is replacing the whole bill instead of most of it.

## How much roof does that take?

The count is limited by usable roof area, not by the bill. Panels need unshaded roof, and the layout has to clear vents, chimneys and the setbacks the fire code requires, which is why the number of panels a roof can hold is a site visit question rather than a spreadsheet one. A house that runs out of room tops out below the coverage its usage would ask for, and the estimate shows that ceiling instead of promising past it.

## Where does the count on this site come from?

Every figure above is computed on the city page from that city's own data: the utility tariff, the sunlight hours, the panel wattage and the performance factor. Two numbers here are third-party checks. The European Commission's PVGIS puts a kilowatt of panels tilted south in Phoenix at about 1,772 kilowatt hours a year, and the Department of Energy's homeowner guide covers how a system is sized against usage. Change the city on this site and the panel count changes with it.
