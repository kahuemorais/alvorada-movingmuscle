## What an estimate answers

A solar estimate answers two questions at once: how much of your electricity bill your roof can cover, and what it costs to get there. Every other figure on the page is a step toward those two answers, and reading them in order is what keeps the last one from looking like a promise.

## Read the system size first

System size is measured in kilowatts, and it is the amount of panel your roof can hold rather than the amount of power you use. The two are often different: a roof that faces away from the sun tops out before it covers the whole bill, and that ceiling is worth knowing before you look at any price.

## Then read what the system is expected to make

Production is what the panels are expected to generate over a year, in kilowatt hours. It comes from the size of the system, the sunlight where you live and the way the panels are angled. It is an estimate and not a meter reading, so treat a small difference between two quotes as noise and a large one as a question worth asking aloud.

## Then the price, and which price it is

A system price is quoted before the federal tax credit and after it, and the two numbers are not interchangeable. Whether you can use the credit depends on how much tax you owe, which is a question for your tax preparer and not for a solar page. Read the line that says which one you are looking at.

## Finally, the bill you are replacing

Compare the estimate against the bill you already pay, at the tariff you already pay. A system that covers most of your usage is still a poor fit if your utility credits exported power at a rate that changes the arithmetic. When the estimate and the bill disagree, the bill is right.

## Where do these figures come from?

Every figure on a city page of this site is computed from that city's own data: the utility tariff, the sunlight hours, the panel used and the installed cost per watt. Change the city and the estimate changes with it, which is the reason there is a page per city instead of one page for the whole country.
