## Did the 30% federal solar credit end?

Yes, for systems placed in service after December 31, 2025. The IRS instructions for Form 5695, the form used to claim residential energy credits, say it in the section that opens the document: "You can't claim residential clean energy credits for expenditures made after December 31, 2025." The 30% rate applied to property placed in service in 2022 through 2025.

## What did the credit cover while it lasted?

Thirty per cent of the cost of qualified solar electric property, and that included the labor properly allocable to the onsite preparation, assembly and original installation, plus the piping and wiring that connect the system to the house. Solar water heating, small wind, geothermal heat pumps and battery storage were on the same list, and fuel cell property carried its own limit of $500 for each half kilowatt of capacity.

## What happens to a 2025 system that could not use the whole credit?

The unused portion carries to 2026. The credit is taken against the tax owed, so a system installed in 2025 with a credit larger than the tax bill leaves the difference for the following year. That is why the instructions tell you to file the form even when the credit cannot be used in the year of the installation.

## What does that change about an estimate?

The price line on a city page of this site subtracts 30%. That is the rate the federal program carried, and it is what the data of the page applies to the calculation. For a system placed in service in 2026, that subtraction does not apply to the install: the federal credit is not available for it, and the figure to weigh against your bill is the price before the credit. The city pages give both numbers, because the answer to how much a system costs states the price before incentives and the price after the credit.

## Is anything left at the state level?

Arizona has a credit of its own, described in the note printed under the estimate on the city page. It is separate from the federal one, it is not part of the calculation, and it changes on its own schedule.

## What should you do with that?

Ask your tax preparer about the year your system goes in. A credit against income tax depends on the tax you owe, and that is not a question a solar page can answer for you. What this site can do is the arithmetic: what the roof produces, what the system costs, and what the bill looks like afterwards.

## Where do these figures come from?

The rate, the dates and the carryforward come from the IRS instructions for Form 5695, which name the 30% rate for property placed in service in 2022 through 2025 and the termination for expenditures after December 31, 2025. The prices and the monthly savings come from the city page, computed from that city's own data.
